Today’s Focus Question: How can accurate categories help us make better claims about AI systems?

What you will be able to do

  • Define AI, machine learning, automation, algorithm, and computer science in student-ready language.
  • Explain how the terms overlap without treating them as synonyms.
  • Classify a system using evidence rather than buzzwords.

What you need: the Lesson 1.2 reading, a way to create a simple map/table in Canvas, and your five Field Guide candidate systems. No outside account is required.

Lesson sequence

1 Retrieve and predict — 5 minutes

In 2–3 sentences, answer: Which term is broadest? Which two terms are easiest to confuse? Explain why.

2 Experience: Which label fits? — 8 minutes

For each case, choose all labels that are supported: computer science, algorithm, automation, AI, ML, or uncertain. Then write one reason.

  • A calculator adds two numbers.
  • A washing-machine cycle follows a selected program.
  • A spam filter sorts messages as wanted or unwanted.
  • A social-media recommender ranks posts.
  • A hand-written recipe gives cooking steps.
  • A scientist’s simulation program models weather.
  • A self-driving prototype senses roads and selects actions.

Canvas text fallback: Make a table with columns for case, supported labels, labels not supported, and evidence. Multiple labels may be correct. Use “uncertain” whenever the description lacks needed information.

Reasoning check: A timer-controlled process is usually automation and uses an algorithm, but it is not automatically ML. A spam filter may use ML, but you should say “may” unless the evidence tells you how it is built. A system can fit several categories at once.

3 Build the vocabulary map — 10 minutes

In your notes, draw or list these relationships:

  • Computer science is the broadest academic field here.
  • Algorithms appear throughout computing, including AI.
  • Automation describes a process; it can use fixed rules, AI, or neither.
  • AI is a field concerned with intelligent-seeming tasks.
  • ML is one approach within AI that learns patterns from data.

Write one example that fits multiple labels and one that is automation but not ML.

4 Guided Practice 1.2 — 8–10 minutes

Complete the Canvas quiz. You may retake it; read feedback after each response.

  • Put ML, AI, and computer science in a broad-to-narrow relationship.
  • Explain why a fixed thermostat may be automation without ML.
  • Classify a spam filter using evidence.
  • Identify an overclaim in a sample advertisement.
  • Match terms to definitions.

5 Independent application: Concept Map — 15–20 minutes

Open Assignment 1.2 — Concept Map. Create an annotated map, diagram, or table that defines all five terms and shows their relationships.

AI-use guidance: Do not use generative AI to create the content or wording of your map. You may use a reliable non-AI source to verify a factual detail if you cite it. Your submitted explanations must be your own.

6 Build your AI Field Guide — 5 minutes

For each of your five candidate systems, add a preliminary label set: AI? ML? automation? algorithm? uncertain? Write 1–2 sentences of evidence-based justification. You may revise these labels later.

Success checklist

  • I can define each term without using the others as a synonym.
  • I can explain at least two overlaps.
  • I can identify what evidence is missing before calling a system ML.
  • I completed Guided Practice 1.2 and read the feedback.
  • I submitted a concept map and updated my Field Guide labels.

AI Lab Notebook entry

Submit 4–6 sentences: Which term was easiest to misuse before this lesson? Describe one vague “AI” advertisement or headline. Add: No AI assistance used or name the tool and its specific limited purpose.
