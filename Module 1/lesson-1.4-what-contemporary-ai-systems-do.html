Today’s Focus Question: What can a system X-ray reveal that a product label hides?

What you will be able to do

  • Identify common task categories for contemporary AI systems.
  • Describe a system in terms of input, output, task, data, and human choices.
  • Distinguish observable capabilities from unsupported marketing claims.

What you need: the Lesson 1.4 reading and your remaining Field Guide systems. No outside account is required.

Lesson sequence

1 Retrieve and predict — 5 minutes

Choose a navigation app, video recommender, phone camera, chatbot, music app, or online store. In one sentence, state what it is trying to do for the user.

2 Experience: System X-ray — 10 minutes

Analyze these four systems: route planner, photo organizer, music recommender, and customer-service chatbot. For each, identify likely inputs, outputs, task type(s), human decisions, and one limit.

SystemLikely inputsOutputsTask(s)Human choice / limit
Route plannerLocation, destination, map/traffic dataSuggested route, arrival estimatePrediction, planning, recommendationWho chooses route goals? What traffic is missing?
Photo organizerImages and associated informationLabels/groupingsPerception, classificationWho labels training examples? When can it mislabel?
Music recommenderListening history and preferencesRanked songsRecommendation, predictionWhich signals count? Can it narrow choices?
ChatbotPrompt and conversation contextGenerated textGeneration, language processingWho sets rules? Can it be inaccurate?

Canvas text fallback: Copy the table into your notes and complete each blank using cautious language such as likely, may, appears to, or not enough information is provided.

Reasoning check: “The algorithm decided” leaves out human choices about goals, data, success, access, and appeals. A single app may use several task categories.

3 Practice capability versus claim — 8 minutes

Revise this sentence: “Our AI knows exactly what you need.” Your revision must name a task, likely evidence/data, and one uncertainty or boundary.

4 Guided Practice 1.4 — 8–10 minutes

Complete the Canvas quiz. You may retake it; feedback is part of the lesson.

  • Match systems to task categories.
  • Identify a likely input/output pair.
  • Identify a hidden human decision.
  • Revise a marketing claim into a cautious description.
  • Explain why one app may use several tasks.

5 Independent application: Field Guide Entries 3–5 — 15–20 minutes

Open Assignment 1.4 — Field Guide Entries 3–5. Complete three system X-rays using the required fields.

AI-use guidance: You may use reputable sources to verify facts. Clearly label inferences. Do not use generative AI to draft or rewrite your entries.

6 Build your AI Field Guide — 5 minutes

Review all five systems. Mark any claim that still needs evidence or a more cautious wording. Your goal is a Field Guide a reader can inspect.

Success checklist

  • I can name a system’s task rather than only its brand.
  • I can identify plausible inputs and outputs.
  • I can name a human choice and a limitation.
  • I completed Guided Practice 1.4 and read feedback.
  • I submitted Entries 3–5 with evidence or labeled inference.

AI Lab Notebook entry

In 4–6 sentences, identify the part of a system X-ray that was hardest to infer. Revise one claim about a familiar AI product to be more precise. Add your AI-use disclosure.
