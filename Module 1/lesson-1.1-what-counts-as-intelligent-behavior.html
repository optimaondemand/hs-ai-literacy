AI Literacy • Module 1 • Lesson 1.1

What Counts as Intelligent Behavior?

Lesson activity and guided application

Estimated time45–55 minutes
Lesson typeConcept + classification practice
Submit todayA Careful Description
Module projectAI Field Guide: system shortlist
Today’s Focus Question: When does a system’s behavior justify calling it intelligent—and what should we avoid assuming from that label?

What you will be able to do

  • Describe at least four behaviors commonly associated with intelligence.
  • Distinguish fixed automation from systems that sense, represent, decide, or adapt.
  • Explain why “intelligent” is both a useful and contested label.
What you need: the Lesson 1.1 reading, a way to write 150–200 words in Canvas, and the willingness to describe a familiar system carefully. You do not need to create an account for any outside website.

Lesson sequence

1

Retrieve and predict

5 minutes

Return to the prediction you made before reading: name one device or app that seems “smart.” In 2–3 sentences, explain what it does that led you to use that word.

Then answer this question before moving on: What evidence would convince you that a system adapts rather than merely follows a fixed rule?

2

Experience: What is the system doing?

10 minutes

Sort each case into the category that is best supported by the information provided: fixed automation, pattern-based AI, human judgment, or uncertain / need more information. The goal is not to force every example into a simple box. The goal is to notice what evidence is missing.

A. A timer turns classroom lights off at 10:00 p.m.
B. A map app changes a route after detecting traffic delays.
C. A teacher decides whether an excuse for late work is fair.
D. A photo app groups images that may show the same person.
E. A calculator adds two numbers.
F. A chatbot writes an answer to a history question.
Canvas text fallback: Make a four-column table in your notes. Place A–F under the category you think fits best. Next to each letter, write one reason. For at least one example, write: “I need more information about …”

Check your reasoning. A and E are examples of fixed automation as described. B and D likely involve pattern-based processes, although the exact process requires more evidence. C is human judgment. F may involve pattern-based AI, but its fluent output does not by itself establish understanding, accuracy, or wisdom.

3

Use the four-question system check

8 minutes

Choose either the map app, photo organizer, or chatbot from Step 2. Complete the table in your notes. Use cautious language when you are inferring rather than observing.

QuestionYour notes
What does the system receive?List likely inputs.
What does it produce?List likely outputs.
What process seems to connect the two?State what you know and what you infer.
What changes over time?Describe adaptation, updating, or uncertainty.
4

Guided Practice 1.1

8–10 minutes

Complete the Guided Practice 1.1 Canvas quiz. You may retake it. Read the feedback after each response; the explanations are part of the lesson.

  • Identify fixed automation from an example.
  • Choose the best question for distinguishing a fixed-rule system from a learning system.
  • Explain why impressive output does not automatically prove understanding.
  • Match inputs, outputs, and adaptation to examples.
  • Rewrite a vague claim about an app into a cautious, observable claim.
5

Independent application: A Careful Description

15–20 minutes

Open Assignment 1.1 — A Careful Description and submit your response in Canvas. You will choose one everyday system and describe it using the four-question system check.

Do not use AI to generate your submission. You may use a reliable source to verify a factual detail, but cite or link it and write the description in your own words.

6

Build your AI Field Guide

5 minutes

In your AI Lab Notebook, list five candidate systems for your Module 1 AI Field Guide. Include where you encounter each system. Choose systems from at least three settings, such as school, home, entertainment, transportation, shopping, sports, health/fitness, or creative work.

At least one system should be a case you are not yet sure deserves the label “AI.” That uncertainty is useful; you will investigate it later.

Success checklist

  • I can distinguish a fixed rule from evidence of adaptation.
  • I described a system using inputs, outputs, process, and change over time.
  • I avoided assuming that impressive output proves understanding or wisdom.
  • I completed Guided Practice 1.1 and read the feedback.
  • I submitted A Careful Description and added five candidate systems to my notebook.

AI Lab Notebook entry

Submit 4–6 sentences or add them to your recurring notebook assignment:

  1. Which example in the sort was hardest to classify?
  2. What information would help you classify it more confidently?
  3. Did you use AI assistance today? If yes, name the tool and state exactly what it helped with. If no, write: No AI assistance used.

What comes next

In Lesson 1.2, you will separate terms that people often blur together: computer science, algorithm, automation, artificial intelligence, and machine learning. Clear vocabulary will make your Field Guide more accurate.
