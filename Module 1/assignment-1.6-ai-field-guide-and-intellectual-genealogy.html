Complete the Module 1 project: five evidence-based system descriptions, one historical connection, and a written defense.

Assignment question: What can selected AI-related systems do, what shapes their behavior, what are their limits, and how should a responsible user describe them?

Your task

Submit your completed Module 1 AI Field Guide and Intellectual Genealogy. Your guide should be organized so a reader can inspect each claim, see what evidence supports it, and recognize what remains uncertain.

Required components

  1. Five Field Guide entries. Each entry must include: system name/context; task category; inputs; outputs; likely process or data use; human choices; one benefit; one limitation/risk; and citations or labeled inferences.
  2. One history/genealogy note. Connect a chosen system to an earlier development such as symbolic rules/expert systems, statistical learning, neural networks, search, or generative models. Do not claim direct descent unless evidence supports it.
  3. Written defense (400–600 words). Answer all four questions:
    • Which entry required the most careful inference, and why?
    • Revise one claim you initially made after learning more.
    • What can one system in your guide do well?
    • What should a user not assume merely because the system’s output is impressive?

Evidence and responsible language

Separate observation, documentation, credible reporting, and inference. Use phrases such as appears to, likely, the company states, or the available evidence suggests when appropriate. Avoid anthropomorphic claims unless you can explain exactly what evidence supports them.

AI-use policy

You may use reputable sources to verify factual details and must cite them. Clearly label inferences. Do not use generative AI to draft, rewrite, or edit the Field Guide or written defense. At the bottom, add one disclosure line:

  • No AI assistance used. or
  • AI assistance used: [tool] helped me with [specific limited purpose].

Success criteria

  • All five entries include every required field.
  • Task categories, inputs, outputs, and human choices are specific.
  • Benefits and limitations are meaningful and cautious.
  • Sources are cited and inferences are labeled.
  • The genealogy connection is accurate and relevant.
  • The written defense answers all four questions and includes disclosure.
