Today’s Focus Question: How can a claim about AI be specific enough to investigate and modest enough to be honest?

What you will be able to do

  • Distinguish evidence-based claims from vague or anthropomorphic claims.
  • Revise a system description to identify evidence, uncertainty, and limitations.
  • Complete and defend an accurate AI Field Guide entry.

What you need: the Lesson 1.6 reading, your Field Guide draft, and a way to submit a 400–600 word written defense. No outside account is required.

Lesson sequence

1 Retrieve and revise — 5 minutes

Rewrite this claim: “This AI knows what students need and gives perfect advice.” Underline words that need evidence. Your revision must name a task, an evidence source or inference, and a limit.

2 Experience: Annotate the sample entry — 10 minutes

Read the fictional entry below. Identify vague/anthropomorphic language, unsupported claims, missing inputs/outputs, missing human choices, and missing limitations.

“StudySpark understands every learner and always knows the best next lesson. It uses AI to make perfect recommendations, so students can trust every suggestion.”

Canvas text fallback: Submit a numbered list: (1) words that are vague or anthropomorphic; (2) claims needing evidence; (3) missing inputs/outputs; (4) a possible human choice; (5) a limitation; and (6) a 2–3 sentence revision.

Model revision: “StudySpark asks learners to answer practice questions and records response patterns. The company states that it recommends additional activities using those records. This may help organize practice, but it may not account for a learner’s full context, goals, or needs; people choose the goals, data, and oversight rules.”

3 Use the four habits — 8 minutes

  1. Name the task.
  2. Name the evidence.
  3. Name uncertainty.
  4. Name limitations and human roles.

Review your five Field Guide entries. Revise at least one sentence using these habits.

4 Guided Practice 1.6 — 8–10 minutes

Complete the Canvas quiz. You may retake it; feedback is part of the lesson.

  • Identify anthropomorphic wording.
  • Choose the strongest evidence-based revision.
  • Match claims to evidence, inference, or unsupported assertion.
  • Identify a missing limitation or human role.
  • Revise a vague claim about an AI system.

5 Independent application: Module 1 final submission — 20–25 minutes

Open Assignment 1.6 — AI Field Guide and Intellectual Genealogy. Submit five complete entries, a history/genealogy connection, and a 400–600 word written defense.

AI-use guidance: You may use reputable sources to verify facts; cite them. Clearly label inferences. Do not use generative AI to draft or rewrite the Field Guide or written defense.

6 Mastery reflection — 5 minutes

In your AI Lab Notebook, answer: What changed in your definition of intelligence since Lesson 1.1? Name one habit you will carry into the rest of the course when evaluating an AI claim. Add your AI-use disclosure.

Success checklist

  • I can identify vague, anthropomorphic, and unsupported language.
  • I can state evidence, inference, uncertainty, limitations, and human roles.
  • I completed Guided Practice 1.6 and read feedback.
  • I submitted a complete Field Guide, genealogy note, and written defense.
  • I included citations/labeled inferences and an AI-use disclosure.
