Chinese Room Reflection

Make a qualified argument about fluent AI language output, understanding, and evidence.

Estimated time
20–25 minutes
Points
15
Submission type
Canvas text entry
Word count
350–500 words

Assignment question: What did your rule-following experience show, and what can fluent language output reasonably establish?

Your task

Write a 350–500 word private Canvas response. You are not required to solve the philosophy of mind or agree with Searle. You are required to represent the thought experiment and one serious objection fairly, then state a qualified provisional conclusion.

Your response must include

  1. Your experience: what you did successfully in the symbol activity and what you did not possess or know.
  2. Searle’s central distinction: explain in your own words how syntax, semantics, and behavioral performance relate to the Chinese Room.
  3. One serious objection: explain the systems reply or another thoughtful alternative interpretation fairly.
  4. Your provisional conclusion: state what fluent AI language output establishes and what it does not establish.
  5. Vocabulary: use at least two of these terms accurately: syntax, semantics, behavioral performance, understanding, thought experiment.
Writing support: A strong conclusion can be qualified. For example, you might argue that fluent output establishes an observable capability but does not, by itself, settle every question about meaning, truth, experience, or responsibility.

AI-use policy

Do not use generative AI to generate, rewrite, or edit this response. This reflection measures your individual reasoning. At the bottom, write: No AI assistance used.

Success criteria

  • I accurately explain my experience and Searle’s distinction.
  • I use vocabulary accurately.
  • I represent an objection fairly rather than dismissing it.
  • I make a qualified claim rather than an absolute claim.
  • I meet the word count and include the required disclosure.
