Today’s Focus Question: How can AI history help us judge current claims without either hype or panic?

What you will be able to do

  • Identify major developments in the history of AI.
  • Explain why periods of excitement and disappointment both occurred.
  • Connect a modern system to an earlier technical or intellectual development.

What you need: the Lesson 1.3 reading, access to Canvas text entry, and one credible source for your source card. No outside account is required.

      Canvas text fallback: Make a three-column table labeled hope, demonstrated capability, consequence/limitation. Use the reading’s timeline table. Write one sentence beginning, “This mattered because …” for each event.

      Reasoning check: A historical success can be real without proving that every larger promise was fulfilled. An AI winter is not proof that all AI is useless; it is evidence that claims and evidence did not match.

      10 minutes

      Choose one current AI claim you have encountered. In your notes, complete: “This claim resembles the earlier promise about ___ because ___. The evidence I would need is ___.”

    3. 4

      Guided Practice 1.3

      8–10 minutes

      Complete the Canvas quiz. You may retake it; feedback is part of the lesson.

      • Explain why expert systems were powerful and limited.
      • Define AI winter.
      • Identify changes that helped modern ML advance.
      • Sequence key periods.
      • Explain why historical knowledge improves judgment about current AI claims.
    4. 5

      Independent application: Timeline Source Card

      15–20 minutes

      Open Assignment 1.3 — Timeline Source Card. Choose one milestone and explain its promise, demonstrated capability, limitation, and one credible source.

      AI-use guidance: You may use a reliable source to research your milestone and must cite it. Do not use generative AI to draft or rewrite the source card. Your historical explanation must be your own.

    5. 6

      Build your AI Field Guide

      5 minutes

      Select one of your five systems. Add a short genealogy note that connects it conceptually to rules/expert systems, statistical learning, neural networks, search, or generative models. Do not claim direct descent unless you can support it.
