What Contemporary AI Systems Do

Reading before the lesson activity

Estimated time
15–20 minutes
Focus question
How can a system X-ray reveal what an AI product does, what shapes it, and what it cannot guarantee?
Required for
Lesson 1.4: Field Guide Entries 3–5

Begin with a prediction. Choose a navigation app, video recommender, phone camera, chatbot, music app, or online store. In one sentence, state what you think it is trying to do for the user.

Tasks, not magic

“AI” is not one machine or one ability. Contemporary systems perform different tasks: detecting objects in images, transcribing speech, predicting likely choices, generating text, translating languages, recommending content, or controlling physical devices. Understanding a system begins with describing its task precisely.

Task categoryWhat it doesExample
ClassificationAssigns an item to a category.Spam/not spam; plant species A/B/C.
PredictionEstimates a likely future value or event.Travel time or product demand.
RecommendationRanks options a user may prefer.Suggested songs or videos.
GenerationProduces content.Text, image, audio, code, or video.
PerceptionExtracts information from images, sound, or sensors.Speech transcription or object detection.
Planning/controlSelects actions toward a goal under constraints.Route selection or robot movement.

A single app may combine several tasks. A route planner perceives location data, predicts travel time, searches possible routes, and recommends one. Naming tasks helps us ask better questions about performance and risk.

Perform a system X-ray

A system X-ray looks beneath the product name. Ask: What goes in? What comes out? What task is being attempted? What information or training may be involved? What human choices shape the system? What are the limits?

Inputs, outputs, and human choices

Inputs may include information you provide directly, sensor data, records created by others, or patterns in past behavior. Outputs may be labels, rankings, suggestions, generated content, or actions.

Human choices appear throughout: people choose the goal, decide what data to collect, label examples, set rules, write instructions, define success, decide who receives the system, and choose how users can appeal or override it. “The algorithm decided” is usually incomplete. System behavior is connected to human design and institutional decisions.

Capability versus claim

A marketing claim might say, “Our AI knows exactly what you need.” A careful description might say, “The app ranks products using patterns in past clicks and purchases; it may not reflect a user’s complete needs or interests.” The careful description identifies a mechanism, leaves room for error, and avoids pretending the system has certainty or wisdom.

Careful claim habit: Describe the observable task first. Then name what you know, what you infer, who made relevant choices, and one limitation or unanswered question.

Key vocabulary

  • classification: assigning items to categories.
  • prediction: estimating an unknown or future value from available information.
  • recommendation system: a system that ranks or suggests options based on patterns and criteria.
  • generative system: a system that produces new content from learned patterns and instructions.
  • system X-ray: a structured description of inputs, outputs, task, likely data/process, human choices, and limitations.

As you move to the lesson

Choose one system. Be ready to describe it with inputs, outputs, task categories, human choices, and a limit—not just a product label.
