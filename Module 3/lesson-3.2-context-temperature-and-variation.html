AI Literacy · Module 3 · Lesson 3.2

Context, Temperature, and Variation

Estimated time: 30–45 minutesCanvas Page + PracticeHands-on with an LLM

Today’s target: Analyze how context, instructions, and variation affect generative outputs.
Why it matters: Why can one prompt produce different answers at different times or settings?

Step 1 — Retrieve

In Canvas, answer this warm-up in 2–3 sentences: When have you seen information that looked convincing before you knew whether it was true? What made it convincing?

Step 2 — Experience

Predict, then run it twice

  1. Predict. Write one sentence: if you send the exact same prompt to a language model twice, will you get the same answer? Why or why not?
  2. Run. Open the LLM platform (Arthur Live) and note the model name and its current settings. In a new chat, send this prompt: “Explain how photosynthesis works to a sixth grader in about 120 words. Include one analogy and one specific fact (a number, name, or date).” Copy the output.
  3. Run it again. Open another new chat, change nothing, and send the identical prompt. Copy the output.
  4. Look for the difference. Mark every place the two outputs differ: the analogy, the fact, the word choices, the length. Was your prediction right?
  5. Find the settings. Look for a temperature control (and any others, such as top-p or maximum length). Write down what you find and what the platform says each one does.

What to notice: Which differences were just wording, and which ones changed the facts? Your explanation—not just the click or the copy—is the learning evidence.

Step 3 — Learn

Use the Reading 3.2 page as the core explanation. As you reread, make a three-column note: claim or output, what supports it, and what remains uncertain. This habit prevents a polished response from becoming a substitute for evidence.

Mini-protocol: Stop, trace, decide

  1. Stop: Mark any factual, causal, numerical, or quoted claim that matters to your conclusion.
  2. Trace: Find the strongest available original or credible source. Read beyond the snippet or citation.
  3. Decide: Keep, qualify, revise, or remove the claim. Record why.

Step 4 — Practice with feedback

Match each change you could make to what it controls and what it does not guarantee.

If you change…You are changing…Strong reasoning looks like
The words of the prompt or the context you addWhat the model has to work fromMore context can steer the answer. Context you invent or paste in can also be wrong, so check it.
A source passage you provideWhat the answer can be grounded inCheck that each claim in the answer really appears in the passage.
Temperature (lower or higher)How much the model sticks to its likeliest wordsLower gives more repeatable output. Higher gives more varied output. Neither setting makes a claim true.
A fresh chat versus a continued chatWhether earlier messages are still in viewIf you want a clean comparison, use a fresh chat for every run.

Step 5 — Apply independently

Required task: Two controlled experiments on a real LLM: one that changes only the context, and one that changes only the model settings (such as temperature). Log every run and analyze the differences.

Guiding question: What changed across the outputs, and which changed condition best explains the difference?

Full directions, the run table, and the rubric are in Assignment 3.2.

Step 6 — Check and reflect

Before submitting, answer: What did you verify yourself? What did you revise or reject? What remains uncertain?
