AI Literacy · Module 3 · Lesson 3.4

Prompting as Rhetoric and Specification

Estimated time: 30–45 minutesCanvas Page + Practice

Today’s target: Revise prompts using audience, purpose, context, constraints, format, examples, and verification requirements.
Why it matters: How does a prompt shape the task a generative system is actually being asked to do?

Step 1 — Retrieve

In Canvas, answer this warm-up in 2–3 sentences: When have you seen information that looked convincing before you knew whether it was true? What made it convincing?

Step 2 — Experience

Interactive or text fallback

Prompt compiler: diagnose a weak prompt using a checklist of missing specifications. Accessible fallback: mark the checklist manually and write revision notes.

What to notice: Your first reaction is data about how persuasive presentation can be. Your explanation—not the click or sort alone—is the learning evidence.

Step 3 — Learn

Use the Reading 3.4 page as the core explanation. As you reread, make a three-column note: claim or output, what supports it, and what remains uncertain. This habit prevents a polished response from becoming a substitute for evidence.

Mini-protocol: Stop, trace, decide

  1. Stop: Mark any factual, causal, numerical, or quoted claim that matters to your conclusion.
  2. Trace: Find the strongest available original or credible source. Read beyond the snippet or citation.
  3. Decide: Keep, qualify, revise, or remove the claim. Record why.

Step 4 — Practice with feedback

Practice itemStrong reasoning looks like
“The response sounds certain.”Certainty is a style feature, not evidence. Trace the claim.
“A link appears after the sentence.”Open it and check that it supports the specific claim in context.
“The draft is polished.”Check authorship, source support, and whether the wording overstates what evidence shows.

Step 5 — Apply independently

Required task: Three versions of one prompt, with a 300–400 word explanation of how each revision improves task clarity.

Guiding question: Which missing specification would most improve the weak prompt first, and why?

Step 6 — Check and reflect

Before submitting, answer: What did you verify yourself? What did you revise or reject? What remains uncertain?

Step 7 — Build the module project

This lesson contributes to the Verified Research Brief and Prompt Portfolio. Save your notes, source checks, prompt versions, and decisions. They are evidence of your process and authorship.

Accessibility: Complete the text fallback if an interaction does not work with your device or assistive technology. The fallback meets the same objective and is equally acceptable.
