Sensing is not the same as perceiving

A sensor collects a signal: light at a camera pixel, sound pressure at a microphone, distance from a lidar unit, or motion from an accelerometer. Perception is the process of turning signals into a usable interpretation: “there is a bicycle,” “these words were spoken,” or “this object is moving toward the road.”

Human perception also depends on signals, context, attention, prior knowledge, and conditions. Computer perception differs in methods and limitations, but both can be affected by incomplete or misleading information.

Why conditions matter

A model learns and operates through particular representations. If training examples mostly show objects in clear daylight, it may perform less reliably at night. If an audio system is evaluated with one microphone and one accent pattern, it may struggle elsewhere. A cropped image may remove exactly the evidence a model uses.

These failures do not automatically mean that a system is useless. They identify conditions of use: situations where a system should be tested, monitored, limited, or paired with human review.

Common environmental failure modes

  • Lighting: shadows, glare, dusk, and backlighting change image values.
  • Angle and scale: objects look different close up, far away, or from an unfamiliar viewpoint.
  • Occlusion: a relevant object is partly blocked.
  • Noise: unwanted signals interfere with speech or sensors.
  • Distribution shift: the new environment differs meaningfully from the examples used to build or test the system.
Stress-test habit: Do not ask only “Does it work?” Ask “Under what conditions was it tested, and what changes might make the input no longer resemble the examples it learned from?”

Key vocabulary

  • sensor: a device that records a signal from the environment.
  • perception: interpreting signals into usable information.
  • computer vision: methods that extract information from images or video.
  • occlusion: partial blocking of relevant information.
  • distribution shift: a meaningful difference between conditions/data used before and conditions/data encountered later.
