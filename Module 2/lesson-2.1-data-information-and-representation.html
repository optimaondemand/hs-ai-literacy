Today’s Focus Question: How do observations become data representations that support, but do not replace, judgment?

What you will be able to do

  • Distinguish observations, labels, categories, claims, and decisions.
  • Explain why data are representations rather than complete reality.
  • Identify features and missing context in a familiar process.

Materials: Reading 2.1, Canvas text-entry access, and one familiar app or school process. No external account is required.

1 Retrieve and predict — 5 minutes

Classify these: “72°F,” “hot day,” “the classroom is uncomfortable,” and “open the window.” Which are an observation, category/label, claim, and decision?

2 Experience: data-type sorter — 8 minutes

For each item, mark observation, label, category, claim, decision, or more than one: a photo; “cat” written on a photo; a school’s “on time” rule; a statement that a student is disengaged; a recommendation to contact a family; a count of missed assignments.

Accessible text fallback: Make a six-row table with columns item, type, why, what may be missing. There may be more than one reasonable answer if you explain it.

Feedback check: A label is not the same as a fact about a person. “Disengaged” is an interpretation that may need more context. A decision is an action, not merely a prediction.

3 Learn — 10 minutes

Read Reading 2.1. In your notes, write one example of each term: data, representation, label, feature, and proxy. Add a sentence explaining what each example leaves out.

4 Practice with feedback — 8 minutes

Complete Guided Practice 2.1 in Canvas. You may retake it. Expect items about labels versus claims, features versus people, and missing context.

5 Apply independently — 15 minutes

Open Assignment 2.1 — Data Inventory. Analyze one familiar app or school process using observations, labels, categories, claims, decisions, features, and missing context.

AI-use guidance: Do not use generative AI to draft the inventory. You may consult a product’s documentation for factual details and cite it.

6 Build the portfolio — 5 minutes

List two possible low-risk model contexts you could investigate later (for example: classifying recyclable items from descriptions, sorting plant-leaf descriptions, or identifying music genres from teacher-provided examples). Do not collect personal or sensitive data.

Success checklist

  • I can distinguish a recorded observation from a label, claim, or decision.
  • I can name what a representation leaves out.
  • I completed Guided Practice 2.1 and read feedback.
  • I submitted a Data Inventory with a disclosure.

AI Lab Notebook

Write 3–5 sentences: What is one piece of data a familiar system could collect, and what might that data fail to represent? Add your AI-use disclosure.
