Model Investigation Portfolio Synthesis

Reading before the Module 2 portfolio submission

Estimated time
15–20 minutes
Focus question
What does responsible model investigation look like when evidence, errors, limits, and human choices are considered together?
Required for
Lesson 2.9: Model Investigation Portfolio

Begin with a prediction. Complete the sentence: “A small model may be useful for ___ under ___ conditions, but it should not be used for ___ because ___.”

Portfolio synthesis is not a victory lap

A model investigation portfolio brings together the work of Module 2: representations, perception conditions, learning approach, training evidence, evaluation, error analysis, and responsible documentation. The portfolio is not a claim that you built an expert system. It is evidence that you can investigate how a model works, what it learned from, when it succeeds or fails, and how to describe it honestly.

The evidence chain

ArtifactQuestion it answersWhat a careful reader should see
Experiment planWhat task was investigated and how was it tested?A bounded, safe purpose and deliberate test cases.
Training-data logWhat examples/variation shaped learning?Categories, examples, and what was missing.
Evaluation recordWhat happened on new or varied cases?Expected/actual results and error patterns.
Error/bias analysisWhat might explain a failure?Evidence, uncertainty, a next test, and a boundary.
Model cardHow should someone use or not use this model?Purpose, limits, and qualified claims.

Make claims proportionate to evidence

A portfolio conclusion should connect every claim to evidence. If the model performed well on 12 tests, say that it performed well on 12 tests under specified conditions. Do not turn a small sample into a universal promise. If your model failed, describe the failure and what you learned from it. Negative evidence is evidence.

Questions for a strong synthesis

  • What was the intended task, and why was it appropriate for a small classroom investigation?
  • What representations, labels, or features shaped the model?
  • What did your varied tests reveal that a single accuracy number would hide?
  • What are two meaningful limits, and how do they change appropriate use?
  • What human choices remain responsible before, during, and after model use?

Documentation is part of accountability

Documentation does not remove responsibility; it makes responsibility visible. A good portfolio helps a reader see what the model did, what the investigator inferred, what is uncertain, and who must still make consequential decisions. It also helps you revise your thinking when new evidence appears.

Final Module 2 habit: Describe the task. Show the evidence. Name the error. State the limit. Identify the human responsibility.

Key vocabulary review

  • representation: a form used to stand for an object, event, property, or idea.
  • generalization: useful performance on relevant new cases.
  • distribution shift: a meaningful change between earlier and later conditions/data.
  • proxy: a stand-in measure for something harder to observe directly.
  • model card: documentation of purpose, evidence, limits, and appropriate use.

As you move to the lesson

Gather your experiment plan, lab log, evaluation record, error analysis, and model card. Be ready to identify one claim you revised because the evidence required more caution.
