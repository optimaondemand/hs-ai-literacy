Model Investigation Portfolio

Submit evidence of how you investigated a small model or simulation: its task, data, tests, errors, limits, and responsible use.

Estimated time
30–40 minutes
Points
50
Submission
Canvas text entry or one organized document
Written synthesis
500–700 words

Task: Submit a complete Module 2 Model Investigation Portfolio. You may submit work from an approved no-code model lab or the teacher-provided simulated model path. Your portfolio should help a reader see what happened, not merely what you hoped happened.

Required portfolio artifacts

  1. Experiment plan: purpose, categories, success criteria, 12+ proposed test cases, error concerns, and safety boundary.
  2. Training-data log: category definitions, examples/variation, and what was missing.
  3. Evaluation record: 12+ test cases with expected/actual results, including errors.
  4. Error and bias analysis: evidence, at least two plausible upstream causes, uncertainty, next test, and boundary.
  5. Model card: purpose, data context, evaluation, limitations, appropriate/inappropriate uses, and responsible claim.
  6. Written synthesis (500–700 words): respond to all prompts below.

Written-synthesis prompts

  1. What narrow task did you investigate, and why was it appropriate for a small classroom investigation?
  2. What representations, labels, or features shaped your model/simulation?
  3. What did your varied tests reveal that one accuracy number would hide?
  4. Describe one success pattern, one error pattern, and two meaningful limits.
  5. Revise one earlier capability claim to make it proportional to your evidence.
  6. What is one appropriate use, one inappropriate use, and one human responsibility that remains?
Integrity rule: Do not invent evidence. If you did not complete a live training run, use the simulated-model records honestly. If you did not run a test, identify it as a limitation and state what a future test would examine.

AI-use disclosure

Do not use generative AI to draft, rewrite, or edit the portfolio or written synthesis. You may use your own course artifacts and teacher-provided simulation materials. Add a final disclosure line: No generative AI assistance used, or identify a tool and its specific limited purpose.

Success criteria

  • All five artifacts are present and internally consistent.
  • Evidence separates observation, inference, and uncertainty.
  • Evaluation includes varied tests and honest error reporting.
  • Limits, appropriate use, inappropriate use, and human responsibility are explicit.
  • The synthesis answers all prompts, meets length, and includes disclosure.
