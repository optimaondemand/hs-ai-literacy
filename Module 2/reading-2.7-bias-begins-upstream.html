Bias Begins Upstream

Reading before the lesson activity

Estimated time
15–20 minutes
Focus question
How can sampling, labeling, measurement, proxies, and missing data shape a model before it makes a prediction?
Required for
Lesson 2.7: Error and Bias Analysis

Begin with a prediction. A plant classifier is trained mostly on close-up photos taken in sunny weather. What group of future examples may be underrepresented, and what kind of error might follow?

Bias is not only a final output problem

When people notice an unfair or unreliable model output, they may look only at the model’s last step. But patterns often begin earlier: which examples were collected, who or what was left out, how labels were assigned, what measurement was used, which proxy stood in for a harder question, and how a system is deployed.

Five upstream sources

SourceQuestion to askExample risk
SamplingWho/what is represented in the data?Mostly daylight images; weak performance at night.
LabelingWho defines the target and applies labels?Inconsistent “helpful/not helpful” labels.
MeasurementWhat is measured and how accurately?A noisy sensor treated as exact.
ProxyWhat stand-in is used for a harder concept?Clicks used as a stand-in for genuine interest.
Missing dataWhat relevant conditions or groups are absent?No examples of rare but important cases.

Underrepresentation and label skew

Underrepresentation occurs when relevant examples are too few or missing. Label skew occurs when labels are uneven, inconsistent, or shaped by a biased rule. A system may then show different error patterns across conditions or groups. Finding a difference does not automatically reveal one simple cause; it creates a reason to investigate data, labels, measurement, context, and deployment.

Proxies need humility

Many important ideas are difficult to measure directly: understanding, need, risk, merit, attention, or success. A system may use a proxy because it is available. A proxy can be useful for a narrow purpose, but it should not silently become a moral or factual verdict about a person.

Mitigation is investigation, not a magic fix

Possible responses include collecting more varied examples, reviewing labels, improving measurement, narrowing claims, changing thresholds, adding human review, or deciding that a task is not appropriate for automation. Every response should be tested; “debiasing” is not a one-time guarantee.

Upstream habit: When an output seems wrong, ask what pathway produced it: sampling, labeling, measurement, proxy choice, missing data, model design, or use context.

Key vocabulary

  • sampling: how examples are selected or collected.
  • underrepresentation: too few or missing relevant examples.
  • label skew: uneven or systematically distorted labels.
  • measurement error: inaccuracy or mismatch in what is recorded.
  • proxy: a stand-in measure for something harder to observe directly.

As you move to the lesson

Be ready to trace one error back to at least two possible upstream causes without assuming that one cause explains everything.
