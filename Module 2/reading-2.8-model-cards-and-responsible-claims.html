Model Cards and Responsible Claims

Reading before the lesson activity

Estimated time
15–20 minutes
Focus question
How can a model card make purpose, evidence, limits, and appropriate use visible?
Required for
Lesson 2.8: One-Page Model Card

Begin with a prediction. Compare: “Our AI understands every learner” and “This model sorts teacher-provided practice responses into two categories under the conditions tested.” Which claim could you investigate, and what evidence would you need?

A model card is a truthfulness tool

A model card is a short document that describes a model or system clearly enough for others to understand its purpose, data, evaluation, limitations, and appropriate uses. It does not make a system safe by itself. It makes claims inspectable and creates a place to state what is unknown.

Core sections

SectionQuestions it answers
Purpose and taskWhat narrow problem is this model intended to address?
Data/training contextWhat examples, sources, categories, or simulation conditions were used?
EvaluationWhat tests were run? What worked and what errors occurred?
LimitationsWhere is evidence weak? What conditions may degrade performance?
Appropriate useWhen may a user rely on it cautiously?
Inappropriate useWhat should this model not decide or be used for?

Claims should match evidence

Inflated claim: “The model recognizes any plant instantly.” Responsible claim: “In our small test, the model classified five teacher-provided plant categories under the conditions tested. It often failed on distant, partly occluded, or low-light examples. It should not be used for safety, medical, or expert identification.”

Responsible language includes in our test, appears to, under the conditions tested, may, and not enough evidence to claim. Such language is not weakness; it is proportional to evidence.

Appropriate-use boundaries

Every model should have limits. A simple classroom classifier may support learning about data patterns. It should not be used to make judgments about people, diagnose conditions, decide access to opportunities, or replace expert assessment. Boundaries protect users from claims the system cannot support.

Claim-strength habit: State the task, evidence, and conditions before stating capability. Then state at least one limit and one inappropriate use.

Key vocabulary

  • model card: documentation describing a model’s purpose, data, evaluation, limits, and appropriate use.
  • limitation statement: a clear account of conditions or uses where evidence is weak or performance may fail.
  • appropriate use: a bounded context where a model may be used with suitable oversight.
  • inappropriate use: a use the model should not support because of limits or risk.
  • qualified claim: a claim stated with evidence, conditions, and uncertainty.

As you move to the lesson

Be ready to revise one inflated claim into a claim that a reader could test, question, and use responsibly.
