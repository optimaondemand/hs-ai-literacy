Model Cards and Responsible Claims

Lesson activity and documentation practice

Estimated time
45–55 minutes
Lesson type
Documentation + claim revision

Portfolio model-card artifact

Today’s Focus Question: How can documentation prevent a small model from being described as more capable than evidence supports?

Objectives

  • Identify the required sections of a model card.
  • Revise inflated claims into qualified claims.
  • Write purpose, data, performance, limits, and use boundaries for a model.

Materials: Reading 2.8 and your experiment records/simulation. No external account is required.

1 Retrieve and revise — 5 minutes

Rewrite: “My model knows every category perfectly.” Your revision must state a task, evidence/conditions, and one limitation.

2 Experience: claim-strength editor — 10 minutes

Revise each statement: “Our AI is unbiased”; “This model can identify anything”; “The system makes the right choice.” For each revision, name evidence, uncertainty, and a boundary.

Accessible text fallback: Use a three-column table: inflated claim, evidence needed, qualified revision. The table is the complete activity.

Feedback check: A qualified claim is not a vague disclaimer. It gives a precise task and conditions: “In 12 tests under X conditions, the model did Y; it failed on Z.”

3 Learn — 10 minutes

Read Reading 2.8. Use your lab log to outline the six model-card sections: purpose, data, evaluation, limitations, appropriate use, inappropriate use.

4 Practice with feedback — 8 minutes

Complete Guided Practice 2.8 in Canvas. It asks you to identify missing sections, choose evidence-based claims, and distinguish appropriate from inappropriate uses.

5 Apply independently — 15 minutes

Open Assignment 2.8 — One-Page Model Card. Draft documentation for your model or the provided simulation.

AI-use guidance: Do not use generative AI to write your model card. You may use your own lab records and course templates.

6 Build the portfolio — 5 minutes

Save your model card. It is a required portfolio artifact and a reusable documentation habit for later projects.

Success checklist

  • I describe a narrow task rather than a broad ability.
  • I list data/evaluation evidence and conditions.
  • I state limitations and inappropriate uses.
  • I submitted a one-page model card with disclosure.

AI Lab Notebook

Write 3–5 sentences: Which claim in your model card was hardest to qualify, and what evidence would make it stronger? Add disclosure.
