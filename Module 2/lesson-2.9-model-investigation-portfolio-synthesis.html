Today’s Focus Question: What evidence supports a responsible claim about a model’s capabilities and limits?

Objectives

  • Assemble a complete, inspectable model investigation portfolio.
  • Connect data, tests, errors, limits, and use boundaries in a written synthesis.
  • Revise one earlier claim to make it proportional to evidence.

Materials: Your 2.4–2.8 artifacts or the simulated-model equivalents, Reading 2.9, and Canvas submission access. No external account or peer review is required.

1 Retrieve and revise — 5 minutes

Write one capability claim you made earlier. Revise it using this sequence: task → evidence/conditions → error or uncertainty → appropriate-use boundary.

2 Experience: portfolio evidence audit — 10 minutes

Check whether you have an artifact for each question: What task? What data? What tests? What errors? What plausible upstream cause? What limit? What human responsibility? Mark each as present, needs revision, or missing.

Accessible text fallback: Use a seven-row checklist with the questions above. For every “needs revision” or “missing,” write one concrete action you can take before submission.

Feedback check: Do not add invented evidence to fill a gap. If a test was not run, state that limitation and explain what a future test would need to examine.

3 Read and synthesize — 10 minutes

Read Reading 2.9. In notes, write a four-sentence evidence chain: (1) task and representation; (2) training/testing evidence; (3) error/limit; (4) appropriate use and human responsibility.

4 Practice with feedback — 8 minutes

Complete Guided Practice 2.9 in Canvas. It asks you to identify evidence-based portfolio claims, missing documentation, and appropriate-use boundaries.

5 Apply independently — 20–30 minutes

Open Assignment 2.9 — Model Investigation Portfolio. Submit your portfolio artifacts and a 500–700 word written synthesis. You may use the teacher-provided simulated model evidence if you did not use a live tool.

AI-use guidance: Do not use generative AI to draft, rewrite, or edit the portfolio synthesis. Use your own artifacts, records, and course materials. Clearly disclose any limited tool use.

6 Mastery reflection — 5 minutes

In your AI Lab Notebook, answer: What changed in how you judge a model claim after investigating data and errors? Name one documentation habit you will carry into Module 3. Add your disclosure.

Success checklist

  • I included each required artifact or honestly noted a limitation.
  • I show evidence from training, tests, and error analysis.
  • I revise one claim to be more accurate and bounded.
  • I state an appropriate-use boundary and human responsibility.
  • I submit a complete portfolio with disclosure.

What comes next

Module 3 extends this investigation to language, generative systems, prompts, and verification. You will keep using the same habits: inspect inputs, test outputs, name limits, and retain human responsibility.
