Today’s Focus Question: What evidence would show whether a model works, fails, or needs revision?

Objectives

  • Explain the purpose of a train/test split.
  • Interpret true/false positives and negatives.
  • Draft test cases and success criteria for a small model investigation.

Materials: Reading 2.4 and Canvas text-entry access. No calculator or external account is required.

1 Retrieve and predict — 5 minutes

A model is correct 90% of the time. Write two questions you would ask before trusting the number.

2 Experience: confusion-matrix explorer — 10 minutes

Use this scenario: 20 messages actually need prompt attention; 80 are routine. A model marks 18 urgent messages correctly, marks 8 routine messages urgent by mistake, misses 2 urgent messages, and correctly marks 72 routine messages. Identify TP, FP, FN, and TN. Then explain which error you would investigate first and why.

Accessible text fallback: Build the 2×2 table from the reading and place the four numbers: TP 18, FP 8, FN 2, TN 72. The table and written explanation are the full activity.

Feedback check: Accuracy here is 90%, but it does not answer every question. False positives may create extra work; false negatives may delay attention. Context decides which tradeoff deserves attention.

3 Learn — 10 minutes

Read Reading 2.4. Define accuracy, precision, recall, overfitting, and generalization in your notes. Do not memorize formulas without connecting them to error types.

4 Practice with feedback — 8 minutes

Complete Guided Practice 2.4 in Canvas. It includes train/test decisions, confusion-matrix cells, and error-cost questions.

5 Apply independently — 15–20 minutes

Open Assignment 2.4 — Model Experiment Plan. Propose a low-risk model investigation, success criteria, and at least 12 test cases. You may use the teacher-provided simulated model path later if a live tool is unavailable.

AI-use guidance: Do not use generative AI to draft the plan. You may use course materials to refine criteria and test cases.

6 Build the portfolio — 5 minutes

Save your plan. It becomes the experiment-plan artifact in your Model Investigation Portfolio.

Success checklist

  • I can explain why training and testing use different examples.
  • I can identify TP, FP, FN, and TN.
  • I can name which error matters more in a given context and why.
  • I submitted a plan with 12 test cases and disclosure.

AI Lab Notebook

Write 3–5 sentences: Which kind of error would be most concerning in your possible model context? What test case could reveal it? Add disclosure.
